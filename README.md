# Starship // Drift

A playable pixel art space adventure about building a modular ship, managing its crew, and surviving unpredictable encounters. [Play Starship // Drift](https://bishoppawn1.github.io/starship/).

Ships, crew, equipment, pirates, and broken ship wrecks are drawn as detailed pixel sprites. Weapons use slim steel barrels and red receiver panels, and ship interiors use muted metal tones. During combat, high-resolution artwork rotates as a single image on a display-density canvas, keeping the pixel details intact without coarse stepping at different headings.

## Play

Open `index.html` in a browser, or serve this directory with any static file server. No build step or dependencies are needed. Progress saves automatically in your browser.

- The main menu offers **New Game**, **Continue Game** when a local save exists, and **Quit Game**. Starting over asks before replacing a saved run. **Main Menu** in the game saves progress and returns to the title screen; leaving during combat abandons that unresolved encounter. In a browser, Quit Game displays a closing screen because the tab itself remains under your control.
- The starter ship has mirrored engines and two forward-facing pulse cannons, with a spare ion blaster in its hold. In the normal ship view, click rooms to inspect, upgrade, repair, or adjust weapons. The module-placement catalog appears only while trading. Hull construction has no fixed boundary: scroll over the shipyard to zoom around the pointer, use the shipyard arrows or arrow keys to pan, and press **Center Ship** to return to your hull. Negative grid coordinates are valid.
- At a trader, browse the familiar module cards, filter **Weapons**, **Systems**, **Shields**, or **Crew**, and **Buy** a part. Rotate with **Q / E**, then click beside your ship to install it; hovering over an edge room snaps the preview to nearby free cells. **Install Held** uses a stored part. Uninstalled purchases remain in your hold. **Dock at Trader** reopens a trader at your current beacon. The hover preview shows the weapon mount and firing arc. Select an installed room and use **Move Room** to move or rotate it for free; upgrades, damage, and crew are preserved. **Escape** cancels. The ship must stay connected, with clear exhaust and weapon arcs.
- Traders carry **compact (1 cell / 110 thrust)**, **standard (3 cells / 270 thrust)**, and **heavy (6 cells / 620 thrust)** ion engines. Compare their footprints and base thrust on the cards; the inspector shows current force after power, upgrades, and crew bonuses. Rotate each engine to choose its thrust direction, and leave its exhaust clear.
- Recruit named crew from a trader’s **Crew** filter, then give them a job in **Crew Assignments**. Starting crew and recruits can pilot, operate engines, weapons or shields, repair, deliver ammo, or stand by. They walk to their consoles before bonuses apply. Engine operators improve thrust; a pilot enables evasion; shield operators speed recharge. Purchases, identities, and jobs survive saving and returning to a trader.
- Open the **Sector Map** and choose any connected beacon. Routes go both ways, so you can revisit explored locations; cleared encounters do not pay out again. Traders retain limited stock on return visits. Reach the exit and choose **Go to Next Sector** when ready. No fleet chases you. Beacons include pirate encounters, freighter rescues, salvage, derelicts, ion storms, relays, minefields, lifeboats, and outposts. Lifeboat survivors earn a reward when delivered to an outpost; ammo crates supply the next battle; restored relays add two-way shortcuts. A hauler tow or medical rescue can add crew, while derelict ships can yield working weapons for your hold. Traders no longer pay for survey data you have not gathered.
- In combat, **left click your ship** to select it, **scroll** to set its desired arrival angle, and **right click** the arena to move. Q / E and the on-screen buttons also change the desired angle. On touch screens, tap the ship and then tap a destination. The much larger battlefield scrolls with your ship, keeping ships readable as you travel. The ship takes time to turn: each engine applies force in its mounted direction and torque from its position. Engines fire only when the flight controller needs thrust. You can order a destination close to broken ship wrecks; the route keeps a small clearance beyond the ship's collision radius. Ramming one makes the moving ship bounce away without collision damage.
- Pirate ships have separate command, weapon, engine, and armor rooms. Projectiles damage the room they hit. A destroyed weapon stops firing, a destroyed engine stops moving, and a destroyed command core defeats the ship.
- The **command core supplies power automatically and evenly** to active weapons, engines, and shields. **System Power** shows each room’s performance percentage. More systems sharing limited output means slower weapon reload, weaker thrust, and slower shield recharge. Partial damage does not limit power. Use **Upgrade Core Power** outside combat to improve the shared supply. Pilots and engine operators still improve evasion and thrust.
- Shields have rechargeable **strength**, such as **100 / 100**, rather than hit layers. Hits subtract their damage, and excess damage passes through to the hull. Wide shields cover the whole ship; focused shields have more strength and faster recharge over a 105° arc. Lower power slows recharge, while a shield operator speeds it up. Traders carry both designs.
- There are **20 weapon types**, including cannons, lasers, railguns, guided missiles, torpedoes, and weapons that slow, burn, explode, chain, or pierce. Traders draw five weapon units with replacement, so several copies of one weapon may appear while another type is absent. Each market also offers all three engine sizes, shields, a generator, another support-system type, and recruits; returning does not restock sold items. See the [full weapon roster](spec.md#weapon-roster).
- Select any weapon to set its **range limit**. Corner-mounted guns have two mount directions: use **MOUNT** at the trader or **TURN MOUNT 90°** in the inspector. Long-barrel guns stay fixed along the room direction.
- Except for proton lances, weapon overlays and shots stop at your ship’s hull; blocked arcs never resume on the far side. Construction prevents completely obscured conventional weapons, including when adding rooms around an existing weapon. Targets and projectiles must stay within the configured range. The proton lance passes harmlessly through your own sections and stops at the first enemy. Cannons and missiles consume ammo; lasers, ion blasters, and other electrical weapons are ammo-free. Ammo cost appears on weapon cards and in the inspector. Guided missiles automatically acquire targets, launch clear of your hull before tracking, and explode against destructible wrecks. Ion hits temporarily halve the struck system’s efficiency. Disruptors apply a wider, weaker 20% efficiency penalty to nearby systems. Tesla coils lightly damage up to four systems on one ship, losing 30% damage at each bounce.

## Crew orders, boarding and drones

Click a crew portrait or a person on your ship, then **right click a floor cell** to move them there. This pauses their job; use **Resume Assigned Job** or change their assignment to resume automatic work. **Escape** or **Select My Ship** returns to ship selection. Crew have 100 health, take damage when their room is hit, and regenerate 3 HP/s at home after five seconds without damage. Death is permanent for that crew member.

Buy a **Crew Teleporter** at a trader to send a selected crew member and a nearby teammate aboard an enemy within 600 units. They must reach the pad before transport. Select a boarder to follow them, right click enemy floors or use room buttons, and **Recall Crew** once the teleporter recharges. Defenders fight back; boarders sabotage rooms and can capture ships. Surviving boarders return at encounter end.

Buy **Drone Control** to deploy automatic **combat**, **defense**, **repair**, or **boarding** drones during battle. Each deployment costs a drone part and uses a bay slot. Drones need their bay powered; disabled bays pause them. New saves and older saves receive five parts, and traders sell limited refill packs. The control panel shows targets, health, capacity and dismissal. These mechanics use FTL-style autonomous roles and deployment costs.

## Gameplay checks

Run `node --test tests/*.test.cjs` to verify starter symmetry and save migration, trader-only purchases and installation, all 20 weapons, encounter map connectivity, pirate room damage, shield interception, wide wreck clearance and collision bounce, projectile range, rendering, trader room moves, safe missile launches, wreck destruction, weapon effects, unbounded construction and shipyard zoom and panning, recruit persistence, repeated trader weapon stock, equal command-core power sharing, upgrade migration, power-scaled performance, crew engine bonuses, piloting evasion, shield strength, damage overflow, crew injuries and regeneration, direct crew orders, boarding combat and recall, drone deployment costs, autonomous drone roles, and drone-part persistence. No packages are required.

Weapons now have varied packing shapes: six fit in one or two cells, while launchers, magazines, coils, and long accelerators use larger rooms. The trader shows each footprint beside its gun sprite. Existing ships keep their old room shapes; **Refit to New Footprint · Free** at a trader adopts a new layout after you confirm a valid position. Gatling, Gauss, and swarm weapons use timed bursts; scatter and flak salvos fire together. Behavior details on cards and inspectors come directly from the combat settings.
