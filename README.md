# Starship // Drift

A playable pixel art space adventure about building a modular ship, managing its crew, and surviving unpredictable encounters. [Play Starship // Drift](https://bishoppawn1.github.io/starship/).

Ships, crew, equipment, pirates, and broken ship wrecks are drawn as detailed pixel sprites. Weapons use slim steel barrels and red receiver panels, and ship interiors use muted metal tones. During combat, high-resolution artwork rotates as a single image on a display-density canvas, keeping the pixel details intact without coarse stepping at different headings.

## Play

Open `index.html` in a browser, or serve this directory with any static file server. No build step or dependencies are needed. Progress saves automatically in your browser.

- The main menu offers **New Game**, **Continue Game** when a local save exists, and **Quit Game**. Starting over asks before replacing a saved run. **Main Menu** in the game saves progress and returns to the title screen; leaving during combat abandons that unresolved encounter. In a browser, Quit Game displays a closing screen because the tab itself remains under your control.
- **New Game** opens a three-ship lineup: **Wayfarer** (ammo-free pulse cannons), **Arsenal** (missile racks and two upgraded missile assemblies), or **Bulwark** (railguns and two heavy-ammo assemblies). Every starter has two forward engines, two reverse thrusters and a spare ion blaster. Arsenal and Bulwark begin with loaded magazines and six assigned crew: two gunners, two loaders, a pilot and a repairer. Their nearby powered factories can keep both staffed weapons supplied during sustained combat. Continue preserves your existing ship. In the normal ship view, click rooms to inspect, upgrade, repair, or adjust weapons. The module-placement catalog appears only while trading. Hull construction has no fixed boundary: scroll over the shipyard to zoom around the pointer, use the shipyard arrows or arrow keys to pan, and press **Center Ship** to return to your hull. Negative grid coordinates are valid.
- At a trader, browse the familiar module cards, filter **Weapons**, **Systems**, **Shields**, or **Crew**, and **Buy** a part. Rotate with **Q / E**, then click beside your ship to install it; hovering over an edge room snaps the preview to nearby free cells. **Install Held** uses a stored part. Uninstalled purchases remain in your hold. **Dock at Trader** reopens a trader at your current beacon. The hover preview shows the weapon mount and firing arc. Drag any installed room to move it, or use **Rearrange Ship · Free** above the trader catalog, click a room, then click its new position. Touch screens use the same two taps. Q/E rotate; F flips weapon rooms. **Cancel Room Move** or **Escape** cancels. The inspector also keeps **Move Room**; upgrades, damage, and crew are preserved. The ship must stay connected, with clear exhaust and weapon arcs.
- Traders may carry **compact (1 cell / 110 thrust)**, **standard (3 cells / 270 thrust)**, or **heavy (6 cells / 620 thrust)** ion engines. Compare their footprints and base thrust on the cards; the inspector shows current force after power, upgrades, and crew bonuses. Rotate each engine to choose its thrust direction, and leave its exhaust clear.
- Recruit named crew from a trader’s **Crew** filter, then give them a job in **Crew Assignments**. Starting crew and recruits can pilot, operate engines, weapons or shields, repair, deliver ammo, or stand by. They walk to their consoles before bonuses apply. Engine operators improve thrust; a pilot enables evasion; shield operators speed recharge. Purchases, identities, and jobs survive saving and returning to a trader.
- Open the **Sector Map** and choose any connected beacon. Beacons spread across a two-dimensional chart with branching routes and loops. Routes go both ways, so you can revisit explored locations; cleared encounters do not pay out again. Traders retain limited stock on return visits. Reach the exit and choose **Go to Next Sector** when ready. No fleet chases you. Beacons include pirate encounters, freighter rescues, salvage, derelicts, ion storms, relays, minefields, lifeboats, and outposts. Lifeboat survivors earn a reward when delivered to an outpost; ammo crates immediately add eight heavy rounds and four missile rounds to persistent stores; restored relays add two-way shortcuts. A hauler tow or medical rescue can add crew, while derelict ships can yield working weapons for your hold. Traders no longer pay for survey data you have not gathered.
- In combat, **left click your ship** to select it, **scroll** to set its desired arrival angle, and **right click** the arena to move. Q / E and the on-screen buttons also change the desired angle. Use the **− / +** battle zoom controls or **Shift-scroll** over the arena to zoom out and in. On touch screens, tap the ship and then tap a destination. The much larger battlefield scrolls with your ship. The ship takes time to turn: each engine applies force in its mounted direction and torque from its position. Steering uses either turn direction, brakes its rotation, and uses reverse thrusters to slow down without repeatedly turning around; move orders settle at their destination and selected arrival angle. Engines fire only when the flight controller needs thrust. You can order a destination close to broken ship wrecks; the route keeps a small clearance beyond the ship's collision radius. Ramming one makes the moving ship bounce away without collision damage.
- Pirate ships have separate command, weapon, engine, shield, and sometimes armor rooms. Projectiles damage the room they hit; railgun and Gauss slugs can continue into a second aligned room after enemy shields drop. A destroyed weapon stops firing, a destroyed engine stops moving, and a destroyed command core defeats the ship. Pirates hold a useful firing distance, strafe around you, retreat briefly when shields are weak, and route around wrecks.
- The **command core supplies power automatically and evenly** to active weapons, engines, and shields. **System Power** shows each room’s performance percentage. More systems sharing limited output means slower weapon reload, weaker thrust, and slower shield recharge. Partial damage does not limit power. Use **Upgrade Core Power** outside combat to improve the shared supply. Pilots and engine operators still improve evasion and thrust.
- Enemy ships carry rechargeable shields and cannon, laser, or guided missile loadouts. Later assault raiders combine two independent batteries. Read the loadout name and shield strength above each ship; disabling its shield room drops the barrier, and disabling a weapon stops that battery. Lasers use no ammo; ammunition-using enemy weapons draw from finite reserves.
- Shields have rechargeable **strength**, such as **100 / 100**, rather than hit layers. Hits subtract their damage, and excess damage passes through to the hull in the same hit: 140 damage against 100 shield strength leaves 40 hull damage. This applies to both player and enemy shields, with overlapping shields absorbing their remaining strength in turn. Wide shields cover the whole ship; focused shields have more strength and faster recharge over a 105° arc. Lower power slows recharge, while a shield operator speeds it up. Traders may carry either design.
- There are **22 weapon types**, including left-to-right sweeping beams, cannons, lasers, railguns, guided missiles, torpedoes, and weapons that slow, burn, explode, chain, or pierce. Traders draw four to eight random module units with replacement from the full catalog. Some types can repeat, and no module category is guaranteed. Markets also offer recruits; returning does not restock sold items. See the [full weapon roster](spec.md#weapon-roster).
- **Sweep Beam** and **Heavy Beam** cut left to right across exposed rooms, hitting each room once per sweep. They deal 18 and 30 hull damage per room, reduced to 6 and 10 against shields. Their rays stop at your hull and wrecks, and obey their firing arcs and range settings.
- Weapons turn their barrels toward targets before firing and keep tracking while reloading. Select any weapon to set its **range limit**. Corner-mounted guns have two mount directions: use **MOUNT** at the trader or **TURN MOUNT 90°** in the inspector. Long-barrel guns stay fixed along the room direction.
- **Ammo production:** buy an **Ammo Assembly** when stocked under a trader's **Systems** filter. Select the room and choose Heavy (four rounds per batch) or Missile (two rounds per batch). Assemblies use shared power only, with no material input or production cost. Each batch takes four seconds at full power; less power slows production, and factories stop when disabled, unpowered, or full. Each run starts with eight heavy rounds and four missile rounds.
- Assign **Deliver ammo** to a crew member. They collect matching rounds from assembly output or recovered stores at the command core, carry up to four rounds visibly, and fill individual weapon magazines. Weapons stop firing when their magazine cannot cover the next trigger. Every magazine holds four triggers; each projectile costs one round. The **Ammo Production & Delivery** panel shows stock, production and magazine levels. Resources and carried rounds persist between battles; no ammunition is created by entering combat. Production and loading also work while preparing your ship and on the map.
- Ammo Holds have been removed. Existing installed holds become armor with the same footprint, position, health and upgrades; uninstalled holds become armor in your inventory. Crew collect factory output at the assembly and salvaged ammunition at the core. The new starter loadouts and extra crew apply to **New Game**; Continue preserves your current ship and crew.
- Resolved encounter choices and victories return directly to the sector map. Read the outcome in the captain's log; use the map's shipyard button whenever you want to modify the ship.
- Except for proton lances, weapon overlays and shots stop at your ship’s hull; blocked arcs never resume on the far side. Construction prevents completely obscured conventional weapons, including when adding rooms around an existing weapon. Targets and projectiles must stay within the configured range. The proton lance passes harmlessly through your own sections and stops at the first enemy. Missiles, swarm launchers and torpedoes need missile ammo; railguns, flak, siege cannons and Gauss repeaters need heavy ammo. Light guns, pulse cannons, plasma projectors, lasers and electrical weapons are ammo-free. Ammo cost appears on weapon cards and in the inspector. Guided missiles automatically acquire targets, launch clear of your hull before tracking, and explode against destructible wrecks. Ion hits temporarily halve the struck system’s efficiency. Disruptors apply a wider, weaker 20% efficiency penalty to nearby systems. Tesla coils lightly damage up to four systems on one ship, losing 30% damage at each bounce.

## Crew orders, boarding and drones

Click a crew portrait or a person on your ship, then **right click a floor cell** to move them there. This pauses their job; use **Resume Assigned Job** or change their assignment to resume automatic work. **Escape** or **Select My Ship** returns to ship selection. Crew have 100 health, take damage when their room is hit, and regenerate 3 HP/s at home after five seconds without damage. Death is permanent for that crew member.

Buy a **Crew Teleporter** at a trader to send a selected crew member and a nearby teammate aboard an enemy within 600 units. They must reach the pad before transport. Select a boarder to follow them, right click enemy floors or use room buttons, and **Recall Crew** once the teleporter recharges. Defenders fight back; boarders sabotage rooms and can capture ships. Surviving boarders return at encounter end.

Build a **Drone Bay** with a clear exterior launch lane and a **Drone Assembly** at a trader. Choose combat, beam, defense, repair, or boarding in the assembly inspector or drone panel before battle. Assemblies build and replace drones automatically without parts or launch costs. Assign **Deliver ammo / drones** or **Deliver drones** to carry completed drones from the assembly to the bay. Only loaded drones launch automatically; repair drones also work inside the ship between battles. Bay capacity, shared power, and the number of assemblies determine how many drones can operate. Combat and beam drones fly to and circle the selected enemy; defense drones protect your own ship. Beam drones sweep for 9 damage per exposed room, reduced to 3 against shields. Surviving drones carry over to the next encounter.

## Gameplay checks

Run `node --test tests/*.test.cjs` to verify starter symmetry and save migration, trader-only purchases and installation, all 22 weapons, encounter map connectivity, pirate room damage, shield interception, wide wreck clearance and collision bounce, projectile range, rendering, trader room moves, safe missile launches, wreck destruction, weapon effects, unbounded construction and shipyard zoom and panning, recruit persistence, repeated trader weapon stock, equal command-core power sharing, upgrade migration, power-scaled performance, crew engine bonuses, piloting evasion, shield strength, damage overflow, crew injuries and regeneration, direct crew orders, boarding combat and recall, automatic drone production, physical crew drone deliveries and cargo recovery, exterior launch clearance, autonomous drone roles, enemy-orbit movement and retargeting, sweeping beam room hits and one-third shield damage, drone persistence, and weapon aiming before firing. No packages are required. Navigation tests cover both turn directions, settled arrival for all starters, mid-flight redirection, wreck routes, and single-direction torque; shield tests cover hits exceeding a full shield and stacked shields. Ammo-chain tests also cover power-only production, typed deliveries, magazine depletion, crate/save migration, starter selection and reverse-thruster clearance, three-minute sustained combat for both ammo starters, Ammo Hold save migration, and direct map returns.

Weapons now have varied packing shapes: six fit in one or two cells, while launchers, magazines, coils, and long accelerators use larger rooms. The trader shows each footprint beside its gun sprite. Existing ships keep their old room shapes; **Refit to New Footprint · Free** at a trader adopts a new layout after you confirm a valid position. Gatling, Gauss, and swarm weapons use timed bursts; scatter and flak salvos fire together. Behavior details on cards and inspectors come directly from the combat settings.
