# Starship // Drift

A playable pixel art space adventure about building a modular ship, managing its crew, and surviving unpredictable encounters. [Play Starship // Drift](https://bishoppawn1.github.io/starship/).

Ships, crew, equipment, and pirates are drawn as detailed pixel sprites. Small wall-side terminals leave station floors open for crew. Weapons use slim steel barrels and red receiver panels, and ship interiors use muted metal tones. During combat, high-resolution ship artwork turns as a single image in a fixed two-ship view, keeping the pixel details intact at different headings.

## Play

Open `index.html` in a browser, or serve this directory with any static file server. No build step or dependencies are needed. Progress saves automatically in your browser.

- The main menu offers **New Game**, **Continue Game** when a local save exists, and **Quit Game**. Starting over asks before replacing a saved run. **Main Menu** in the game saves progress and returns to the title screen; leaving during combat abandons that unresolved encounter. In a browser, Quit Game displays a closing screen because the tab itself remains under your control.
- **New Game** opens a four-ship lineup: **Wayfarer** (ammo-free pulse cannons), **Arsenal** (missile racks and two upgraded missile assemblies), **Bulwark** (railguns and two heavy-ammo assemblies), or **Drones** (combat and beam drones, two assemblies, an exterior bay, laser support, and five crew). Every starter has two forward engines, a full-hull wide shield, and a spare ion blaster. None starts with backward-facing thrusters. Arsenal and Bulwark begin with loaded magazines and six assigned crew: two gunners, two loaders, a pilot and a repairer. Their nearby powered factories can keep both staffed weapons supplied during sustained combat. Continue preserves your existing ship. In the normal ship view, click rooms to inspect, upgrade, repair, or adjust weapons. The module-placement catalog appears only while trading. Hull construction has no fixed boundary: scroll over the shipyard to zoom around the pointer, use the shipyard arrows or arrow keys to pan, and press **Center Ship** to return to your hull. Negative grid coordinates are valid.
- At a trader, browse the familiar module cards, filter **Weapons**, **Systems**, **Shields**, **Drones**, or **Crew**, and **Buy** a part. Rotate with **Q / E**, then click beside your ship to install it; hovering over an edge room snaps the preview to nearby free cells. **Install Held** uses a stored part. Uninstalled purchases remain in your hold. **Dock at Trader** reopens a trader at your current beacon. The hover preview shows the weapon mount and firing arc. Drag any installed room to move it, or use **Rearrange Ship · Free** above the trader catalog, click a room, then click its new position. Touch screens use the same two taps. Q/E rotate; F flips weapon rooms. **Cancel Room Move** or **Escape** cancels. The inspector also keeps **Move Room**; upgrades, damage, and crew are preserved. Rooms may be temporarily disconnected while docked; reconnect every room by an edge before leaving. Clear exhaust and weapon arcs are still required. Saving and continuing an unfinished refit returns you to the trader.
- Traders may carry **compact (1 cell / 110 thrust)**, **standard (3 cells / 270 thrust)**, or **heavy (6 cells / 620 thrust)** ion engines. Compare their footprints and base thrust on the cards; the inspector shows current force after power, upgrades, and crew bonuses. Rotate each engine to choose its thrust direction. Standard engines reserve only the two squares directly behind their center nozzle; rooms can sit beside that plume. Heavy engines need their full three-square exhaust edge clear.
- Select a room in the ship view or at a trader and use **Scrap Room** to recover half its base price. The button shows the refund. Crew, finished ammo and completed drones are recovered; upgrades do not increase the refund. Keep the hull connected outside trader refits. The command core cannot be scrapped, and combat scrapping is unavailable.
- Recruit named crew from a trader’s **Crew** filter, then give them a job in **Crew Assignments**. Starting crew and recruits can pilot, operate engines, weapons or shields, repair, deliver ammo, or stand by. They walk to their consoles before bonuses apply. Engine operators improve thrust; a pilot enables evasion; shield operators speed recharge. Purchases, identities, and jobs survive saving and returning to a trader.
- Open the **Sector Map** and choose any connected beacon. Beacons spread across a two-dimensional chart with branching routes and loops. Routes go both ways, so you can revisit explored locations; cleared encounters do not pay out again. Traders retain limited stock on return visits. Reach the exit and choose **Go to Next Sector** when ready. No fleet chases you. Each sector mixes pirate encounters, freighter rescues, lifeboats, and a random selection of salvage, derelicts, anomalies, relays, minefields, clinics, workshops, and couriers. Lifeboat survivors and sealed courier packages earn a reward when delivered to an outpost. Clinics can heal or recruit crew; workshops can tune systems. Ammo crates add persistent ammunition, restored relays add two-way shortcuts, and derelicts can yield working weapons for your hold.
- In combat, your ship holds position on the left while one or more enemies hold position on the right. The ships exchange fire automatically. Use the facing dial, mouse wheel, or Q / E to choose which side of your ship faces incoming shots and which weapons can fire; turning takes time and depends on engine torque. Click an enemy ship or a live room to focus your weapons there. Missiles bypass shields; other projectiles hit shields and actual rooms, and a destroyed command core ends the battle. There is no movement arena, wreck obstacle, or battle zoom.
- Pirate ships have separate command, weapon, engine, shield, and sometimes armor rooms. Projectiles damage the room they hit; railgun and Gauss slugs can continue into a second aligned room after enemy shields drop. A destroyed weapon stops firing, a destroyed engine removes evasion, and a destroyed command core defeats the ship. Enemy guns select rooms on your hull and fire from their own mounts.
- The **ship reactor supplies power automatically and evenly** to active weapons, engines, and shields. **System Power** shows each room’s performance percentage. More systems sharing limited output means slower weapon reload, weaker turning torque, and slower shield recharge. Partial damage does not limit power. Use **Upgrade Reactor** in System Power from the ship view, sector map, or trader outside combat to improve supply. This is separate from **Reinforce Room** in the command-room inspector: room upgrades add hull health, and reactor upgrades add power. Pilots and engine operators still improve evasion and turning.
- Enemy ships carry rechargeable shields and cannon, laser, or guided missile loadouts. Later assault raiders combine two independent batteries. Read the loadout name and shield strength above each ship; disabling its shield room drops the barrier, and disabling a weapon stops that battery. Lasers use no ammo; ammunition-using enemy weapons draw from finite reserves.
- Shields have rechargeable **strength**, such as **100 / 100**, rather than hit layers. Non-missile hits subtract their damage, and excess damage passes through to the hull in the same hit: 140 damage against 100 shield strength leaves 40 hull damage. This applies to both player and enemy shields, with overlapping shields absorbing their remaining strength in turn. Wide shield arrays protect the entire hull regardless of room position, and expand with the ship. Focused shields project a 120-unit, 105° local sector from their room, with more strength and faster recharge. Their fixed local range makes placement matter: hull outside a focused field remains exposed unless another shield covers it. Lower power slows recharge, while a shield operator speeds it up. Traders may carry either design.
- Select a **Focused Shield** and use **Aim Shield** or the 15° turn buttons to point its sector independently of the room, including during battle. Re-aiming keeps its charge. The focused-shield preview marks its room emitter and local coverage. Combat uses that same field; a buried focused shield cannot protect an outer room outside its sector. Wide arrays keep whole-hull coverage.
- There are **22 weapon types**, including left-to-right sweeping beams, cannons, lasers, railguns, guided missiles, torpedoes, and weapons that slow, burn, explode, chain, or pierce. Traders draw four to eight random module units with replacement from the full catalog. Some types can repeat; weapons, engines and shields stay random. Every shop also offers a **Drone Bay** and **Drone Assembly**, adding either missing room to its stock. Older saved shops gain missing drone offers while preserving sold-out items and prices. Markets also offer recruits; returning does not restock sold items. See the [full weapon roster](spec.md#weapon-roster).
- **Sweep Beam** and **Heavy Beam** cut left to right across exposed rooms, hitting each room once per sweep. They deal 18 and 30 hull damage per room, reduced to 6 and 10 against shields. Their rays stop at your hull and obey their firing arcs.
- Weapons turn their barrels toward targets before firing and keep tracking while reloading. Fixed encounters extend every weapon’s reach across the exchange; saved numeric range settings remain for older ships and previews. Corner-mounted guns have two mount directions: use **MOUNT** at the trader or **TURN MOUNT 90°** in the inspector. Long-barrel guns stay fixed along the room direction.
- **Ammo production:** buy an **Ammo Assembly** when stocked under a trader's **Systems** filter. Select the room and choose Heavy (four rounds per batch) or Missile (two rounds per batch). Assemblies use shared power only, with no material input or production cost. Each batch takes four seconds at full power; less power slows production, and factories stop when disabled, unpowered, or full. Each run starts with eight heavy rounds and four missile rounds.
- Assign **Deliver ammo** to a crew member. They collect matching rounds from assembly output or recovered stores at the command core, carry up to four rounds visibly, and fill individual weapon magazines. Weapons stop firing when their magazine cannot cover the next trigger. Every magazine holds four triggers; each projectile costs one round. The **Ammo Production & Delivery** panel shows stock, production and magazine levels. Resources and carried rounds persist between battles; no ammunition is created by entering combat. Production and loading also work while preparing your ship and on the map.
- Ammo Holds have been removed. Existing installed holds become armor with the same footprint, position, health and upgrades; uninstalled holds become armor in your inventory. Crew collect factory output at the assembly and salvaged ammunition at the core. The new starter loadouts and extra crew apply to **New Game**; Continue preserves your current ship and crew.
- Encounter choices describe your actions and upfront costs. After choosing, a result card explains what happened and lists changes to your resources, ship, crew, and chart. Press **Continue** to return to the sector map. Combat victories and outpost services use the same flow; the captain's log also keeps the outcome.
- Except for proton lances, weapon overlays and shots stop at your ship’s hull; blocked arcs never resume on the far side. Construction prevents completely obscured conventional weapons, including when adding rooms around an existing weapon. The proton lance passes harmlessly through your own sections and stops at the first enemy. Missiles, swarm launchers and torpedoes need missile ammo; railguns, flak, siege cannons and Gauss repeaters need heavy ammo. Light guns, pulse cannons, plasma projectors, lasers and electrical weapons are ammo-free. Missile racks, swarm rockets and torpedoes bypass both wide and focused shields and damage the hull directly without draining shield strength; this applies to enemy missiles and missile splash too. Evasion and defense drones can still stop them. Guided missiles launch clear of your hull before tracking. Ion hits temporarily weaken the struck system; Cryo slows enemy reloads. Disruptors apply a wider, weaker efficiency penalty to nearby systems. Tesla coils lightly damage up to four systems on one ship.

## Crew orders, boarding and drones

Click a crew portrait or a person on your ship, then **right click a floor cell** to move them there. This pauses their job; use **Resume Assigned Job** or change their assignment to resume automatic work. **Escape** or **Focus My Ship** returns to ship selection. Crew have 100 health, take damage when their room is hit, and regenerate 3 HP/s at home after five seconds without damage. Dead crew disappear from the assignment list immediately and stay dead after reload.

Buy a **Crew Teleporter** at a trader to send a selected crew member and a nearby teammate aboard an enemy within 600 units. They must reach the pad before transport. Select a boarder to follow them, right click enemy floors or use room buttons, and **Recall Crew** once the teleporter recharges. Defenders fight back; boarders sabotage rooms and can capture ships. Surviving boarders return at encounter end.

Use the trader’s **Drones** filter to buy a **Drone Bay** and **Drone Assembly**. Both appear through random stock and may be absent. Returning to a trader does not add missing parts or restock sold offers. Install the bay with a clear exterior launch lane. Choose combat, beam, defense, repair, or boarding in the assembly inspector or drone panel, including during combat. Changing the design restarts the next build; completed drones retain their type. The inspector also offers pause/resume. Assemblies build drones in the ship view, at traders, and in combat, in four seconds at full power per drone. Production, delivery, and repair drones pause completely on the sector map. They have no parts or launch cost, and you can pause or resume each assembly in **Drones & Boarding**. Assign **Deliver ammo / drones** or **Deliver drones** to carry completed drones from the assembly to the bay. Bays have no slot limit; every loaded combat drone launches automatically at battle start, while repair drones work inside the ship between battles. Shared power and crew delivery affect build and deployment speed. Combat and beam drones fly to and circle the selected enemy; defense drones protect your own ship, shooting incoming missiles first and other rounds when no missile is in range. They ignore evaded shots and keep their normal 0.8-second firing cooldown. Beam drones sweep for 9 damage per exposed room, reduced to 3 against shields. Your entire surviving fleet carries over to the next encounter. From sector two, enemy **Flak Escorts** may mount a separate anti-drone battery: its rotating turret fires proximity shells at nearby airborne drones. Bursts damage clustered drones; destroying the flak room or exhausting its ammunition stops it.

## Gameplay checks

Run `node --test tests/*.test.cjs` to verify ship building, saves, sector navigation, crew, weapons, shields, drones, boarding, trade, and fixed combat. A browser smoke test also checks the facing control, room targeting, and the full two-ship exchange. Missile tests cover real launcher salvos, direct hull hits through stacked shields, splash damage, crew injuries, unchanged shield charge and hit state, evasion and drone interception. Defense tests cover missile priority, volley sharing, ignored shots, and cooldown/power limits. Room-scrapping tests cover displayed refunds, core/combat restrictions, hull connections, crew and ammunition recovery, and preserved drones through scrapping, replacement bays and save reloads. No packages are required.

Weapons now have varied packing shapes: six fit in one or two cells, while launchers, magazines, coils, and long accelerators use larger rooms. The trader shows each footprint beside its gun sprite. Existing ships keep their old room shapes; **Refit to New Footprint · Free** at a trader adopts a new layout after you confirm a valid position. Gatling, Gauss, and swarm weapons use timed bursts; scatter and flak salvos fire together. Behavior details on cards and inspectors come directly from the combat settings.

Combat shows your ship in its own main monitor, with compact enemy monitors beside it. Two enemies appear side by side on the right. Click a room in either monitor or its contact button to focus fire; the gold border marks the selected enemy. Enemy hulls stay out of your ship’s view, and larger player ships do not shrink the target monitors.
