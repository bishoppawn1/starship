# Starship game specification

## Concept

Starship is a pixel art space adventure played in the browser. The player builds a ship from functional polyomino modules, travels through space, and responds to random events. Events can include fighting pirates, rescuing a freighter, and other encounters that create choices, rewards, or danger.

The game is hosted from this repository on GitHub Pages, so the playable build must work as a static website.

## Core loop

1. Build or upgrade a ship and prepare its crew.
2. Choose a connected destination on a branching sector map.
3. Resolve the event through combat, rescue, or another appropriate action.
4. Earn or spend resources, repair damage, and continue the journey.

## Starting ships

- **New Game** opens a lineup with rendered previews. **Wayfarer** has two ammo-free pulse cannons; **Arsenal** has two missile racks, two level-2 missile Ammo Assemblies and a generator; **Bulwark** has two railguns and two heavy Ammo Assemblies adjacent to their weapon stations; **Drones** is a carrier with combat and beam drones, two assemblies, a clear exterior bay, a laser repeater and a generator. Every design has two standard forward engines and a 100-strength wide shield array covering the whole hull. Starter ships have no backward-facing thrusters; they steer with differential engine thrust and coast when braking. Hulls remain connected, with clear exhaust and weapon mounts.
- Drones starts with five crew: a gunner, pilot, repairer, and two drone delivery workers. Its two drones begin loaded at the bay for automatic combat launch. Further drones require production and physical crew delivery. Its reactor starts at level 2; this and all other starter changes apply to new runs.
- Wayfarer starts with three crew. Arsenal and Bulwark each start with six crew already assigned: two gunners at the weapon consoles, two ammo loaders, one pilot and one repairer. Each new run starts with 90 metal, a spare ion blaster, eight heavy rounds and four missile rounds in command stores. The ammo starters also begin with full weapon magazines. Two nearby factories and two separate loader reservations support sustained fire from both staffed weapons; the Arsenal’s level-2 factories keep missile production ahead of demand. Previewing or canceling the lineup leaves the existing run unchanged; replacing a living saved run still requires the existing new-game confirmation. The selected design, ammunition, magazines and cargo persist. Continue retains existing loadouts and crew; the revised starter crews and factories apply to new runs. Obsolete Ammo Holds migrate to armor with exactly the same occupied cells.

## Ship construction

- Ships are assembled from polyomino-shaped modules on an unbounded integer grid. There is no fixed hull boundary in any direction, including negative coordinates. The shipyard starts with a 12×10-cell viewport. Scrolling the mouse wheel zooms from 40% to 200% around the pointer, changing the visible cell count while preserving world coordinates and placement. Arrow buttons and arrow keys pan the viewport, and **Center Ship** returns to the hull at the current zoom. Art and combat framing follow the actual ship bounds; larger ships are not cropped from the view. Exterior and firing-clearance checks also work outside the original grid.
- Module shapes and placement determine the ship's layout and overall footprint.
- Each module is primarily walkable floor. One or two cells contain its system equipment or crew interaction station. Connected modules have openings for crew to pass through.
- Ship rooms use detailed pixel art with visible floor panels, hull plating, doors, consoles, weapon mounts, engine nozzles, and crew. The assembled ship is drawn into a high-resolution source sprite so its pixel details rotate with the hull as one continuous piece of artwork. The combat view renders at the display’s pixel density with smooth sampling at transformed edges, rather than snapping rotations onto an enlarged pixel grid. The source art retains deliberately placed pixel details.
- Modules have distinct functions, such as weapons and engines. Additional systems can be added as the design develops.
- The ship layout affects weapon arcs, shield coverage, crew routes, room damage, and how large the ship appears in combat.
- Players can upgrade systems, primarily by spending **metal**.
- New modules come from limited trader stock and enter the player's hold. The catalog cards, purchases, rotation controls, and installation grid are available while docked at a trader. The normal ship view provides inspection, weapon settings, upgrades, repairs, and navigation without the placement catalog. Installing a held module costs no extra metal; uninstalled parts stay in the hold until the next trader visit.
- While docked, **Rearrange Ship · Free** sits above the trader catalog. It enables picking up a room with one click and placing it with another, including when crew occupy the clicked cell; it stays active for moving further rooms. Desktop players can also drag an installed room directly, retaining the grabbed-cell offset. Touch players use click-to-pick-up and click-to-place so scrolling remains available. The inspector still offers **Move Room**. Moving and rotating a room is free and preserves its identity, health, upgrades, weapon range, and crew. The original stays installed until a valid position is confirmed; **Cancel Room Move**, the inspector’s **Cancel Move**, Escape, or leaving the trader cancels. Invalid positions preserve the original room and allow trying another location; dropping outside the grid cancels. The toolbar shows the moving room and rotation/flip controls. Buying or installing a held part exits rearrangement mode. While docked, moved rooms can be placed separately and bridge rooms can be moved even when this temporarily splits the hull. Overlap, exhaust, mount, firing-arc and drone-lane clearance rules still apply to each placement. Every room must be edge-connected again before leaving: the departure button stays disabled and all navigation out of the trader is blocked until the hull is connected. The refit toolbar warns while rooms are disconnected. Ending rearrangement or canceling a preview does not undo confirmed moves. Saving, returning to the menu and continuing, or reloading a disconnected refit resumes at the same trader with all rooms preserved. Placement previews show the room, its actual weapon mount and direction, and a hull-clipped firing arc (or launch arc for guided missiles); invalid previews are red.
- Weapon rooms can be mirrored before placement with **Flip Weapon** or **F**; Q/E and the rotation buttons still rotate them. Mirroring reflects the footprint, console, muzzle, mount facing, reserved clearance, art, and firing overlay together. While docked, **Flip Weapon · Free** in the inspector starts a move preview with the mirrored room. The installed room stays unchanged until a valid placement is confirmed; canceling preserves the original. Health, upgrades, range settings, crew, and saved legacy footprints survive a flip. The mirrored state persists in saved games; older rooms remain unflipped.
- Ion engines mount at the outer hull. Standard and compact engines keep the two cells directly behind their central nozzle clear; the standard engine's outer room cells have no exhaust and may have rooms alongside them. Heavy engines keep their full three-cell exhaust edge and a second cell behind the center nozzle clear. Later construction cannot fill those cells, and each plume must reach open space. Weapon mounts also need an exposed outside cell, except proton lances, which can fire safely through the firing ship’s own sections.
- Rooms use shallow wall-side terminals with a small illuminated monitor, keyboard and metal casing. Terminals use an outer wall where possible and a short corner console beside connecting doors, keeping the crew’s station center clear. Assembly and generator machinery sits on a separate room cell. Rotated and mirrored rooms retain a reachable floor station.
- Rooms remain walkable interiors with small control panels. Hull plating and structural ribs occupy a separate border outside the rooms; engine nozzles and weapon barrels sit on that exterior border and rotate with the ship. Weapons use slender barrel silhouettes, charcoal outlines, neutral steel highlights, and muted red receiver panels. Barrel layouts distinguish cannon, rail, laser, rotary, scatter, missile, and energy families. Ship floors and hull plating use muted warm-gray and olive-gray metals; colored lights remain small accents. Trader cards and weapon inspectors show the actual weapon sprite.

## Encounters and combat

- Combat can involve the player's ship against one or multiple enemy ships.
- Combat uses a fixed, two-sided ship exchange inspired by FTL. The player's ship appears on the left and one or more hostile ships appear on the right. Ships do not travel around an arena, ram, or navigate obstacles during combat. The whole exchange fits in view, including larger hulls. Projectiles, beams, shields, drones, crew, and damage still animate between the ships.
- The player can choose the facing angle of their ship with a dial, Q/E, or the mouse wheel. Zero degrees points the bow at the enemy. The ship turns in place over time using torque from its installed engines; if it lacks torque in one direction it may turn the long way. Facing changes which weapons have a clear arc and which hull side receives incoming shots. Weapons fire automatically when powered, loaded, aligned, and unobstructed. Clicking an enemy ship or room prioritizes that target; actual hull geometry still determines the first room hit. The opposing ships face the player and keep fixed positions.
- Battle distance is a presentation choice rather than a travel mechanic: direct-fire and beam weapons can reach opponents in the fixed exchange, while their saved range values remain only for compatibility with older saves and previews. Guided weapons still launch through their mount's clear lane. Enemy ships retain their detailed source sprites and actual room layouts.
- Pirate ships have separate command, weapon, engine, shield, and sometimes armor rooms. Sector-one room health is 130 for command, 85 for weapons, 80 for engines, 120 for armor, and 85 for shields: 500 total for an armored raider, or 380 for a rescue skiff. A later assault raider adds a second 85-health weapon room. Every room gains 12% of its base health for each sector beyond the first, rounded to whole health. Ordinary swept shots strike the first live room on their path. Destroyed rooms stay visible as dark wreckage but no longer block later shots. Destroying a weapon room stops that battery, destroying the engine removes its evasion, and destroying the core defeats the ship even if other rooms remain.
- Every enemy carries a rechargeable full-hull shield: 100 strength on raiders and 60 on skiffs, increasing by 12% of the base per sector beyond the first. Recharge is six strength per second for raiders or four for skiffs, after the same 1.7-second hit delay as player shields. A shield intercepts non-missile player and combat-drone shots before hull rooms, absorbs only its remaining strength, and passes excess damage to the hull. For example, a 140-damage hit on a full 100-strength shield applies 40 damage to the struck hull room in the same impact. Fully absorbed shots do not injure crew, inflict ion/burning effects, or start Tesla bounces. Shielded ships also absorb non-missile splash damage. Destroying or sabotaging the shield room drops the shield immediately; ion disruption of that room slows recharge. Missile racks, swarm rockets and torpedoes pass through all projected shields, damage the first live hull room on their actual path, and leave shield strength, recharge delay and hit flashes unchanged. This applies to both player and enemy missiles and their splash damage. They still consume missile ammunition, can be evaded, and can be intercepted by defense drones. Boarding damage also bypasses projected shields. The enemy's ring and numeric strength label show its state.
- Enemies have cannon, laser, or missile loadouts from the first sector, with mixed laser/missile assault raiders added from sector three. Rescue skiffs in the same encounter use different loadouts. Each actual weapon is drawn on its own room and aims and fires independently from that mount; room damage, ion disruption and core disruption affect its operation. Laser repeaters fire fast energy bolts without ammunition. Missile racks launch safely forward, then home toward the player's ship and cause a visible blast. Cannons obey their mounted arcs and reach across the fixed exchange. Enemy weapon damage is 60% of the corresponding player design plus 0.35 per sector, rounded; reload takes 1.6 times that design's normal reload. Enemies start with 18 + four rounds per sector, paying the corresponding player design’s ammo cost per shot; energy fire continues with empty stores. Evaded guided missiles lose their lock, and defense drones can intercept them. Enemy names identify their loadout; boarding room controls identify the installed weapon.
- Enemy ships remain on their side of the exchange and fire from their actual weapon rooms. Destroyed enemy weapon rooms stop firing; destroyed shield rooms drop their fields; functioning engine rooms provide evasion instead of movement.
- Wide shield arrays protect the entire hull, starting at **100 / 100**, regardless of where their room is installed. Their field is centered on the ship and expands with its hull bounds. Only focused/local fields originate at their room centroid, follow room moves, and rotate with the ship. Focused shields start at **160 / 160**, recharge faster, and cover a local 105° sector extending 120 units (four tiles) from their room in an independently aimed direction. Select a focused shield and use **Aim Shield**, cardinal/diagonal headings or the 15° turn buttons; aiming is available in combat and preserves current charge. Its bearing is relative to the ship and persists separately from room rotation, movement and upgrades. The hangar and placement preview show actual coverage, and the combat arc and projectile interception use that same bearing. Focused fields have fixed local range, independent of ship size or room upgrades; enlarging the hull never enlarges a focused field. An interior focused projector can leave outer rooms exposed, and moving that room relocates its protection. Wide arrays continue to cover those outer rooms from anywhere aboard. Focused previews mark the room emitter; wide previews surround the whole ship. Each preview uses the same geometry as combat. Non-missile enemy shots hit actual live hull cells or the first local field on their swept path; missiles ignore wide and focused fields and continue to the hull. A local field behind an already-struck outer hull cell cannot protect that cell. Each intersected field absorbs its remaining strength in distance order; excess continues along the same path into another intersected field or the actual room. Unrelated fields never lose charge. Excess damage does not jump ahead to hull before reaching it. Splash and minefield protection also use local coverage. Even a partially recharged shield can absorb its remaining strength. Strength capacity grows by 35% of the base per room upgrade and stays independent of the room's partial health and power share. Recharge starts 1.7 seconds after a hit, at 10 strength per second for a wide shield or 16 for a focused shield, multiplied by shared power and a 40% bonus for an operator at its console. Disabled shield rooms stop projecting. The inspector, system panel, and HUD show current strength out of maximum.
- Destroyed systems stop providing their function until repaired. Partial room damage does not reduce their power share; room health and system power are separate.
- Weapon barrels turn smoothly toward their current target at up to 180° per second within their mounted firing arc. They track during reload and wait until aligned before triggering a shot. Queued burst rounds also wait for alignment if the target moves. Mirrored rooms and independent corner mount facings use the same aiming direction. Pirate gun hardware tracks separately from its hull as well.
- Each weapon fires from a visible mount. Its facing and firing arc determine which targets it can hit in the fixed exchange. Selecting a weapon shows its art and firing arc, clipped at the first hull cell in each direction except for the proton lance’s safe passage through its own ship. Hull shadows never resume on the other side of the ship; disabled rooms still block fire.
- Old direct-fire range settings remain in saved ships for compatibility, but combat now gives every working weapon enough reach across the fixed exchange. Firing arcs, hull obstructions, ammunition, power, and weapon alignment still govern whether a shot launches. Guided missile racks and swarm launchers use a clear launch arc and then track their target.
- Corner-mounted weapons, including pulse cannons, can face either of the two exposed sides of their corner cell, independently of room rotation. Players choose the mount direction before installation or switch it in the installed cannon’s inspector. Railguns and other long-barrel weapons keep their fixed barrel direction.
- Except for proton lances, construction rejects weapons with no unobstructed direction from their firing arc to open space, and rejects additions that completely enclose an installed weapon’s firing arc. A mount cannot be switched into a completely blocked direction. Partially blocked arcs remain usable in their clear directions.

## Ship systems and power

- The ship reactor supplies eight power units initially, independently of command-room upgrades. Installed small power generators add to this supply. Power is shared automatically and equally between all active weapon, engine, shield, teleporter, ammo-assembly, drone-bay and drone-assembly rooms; each reaches full performance at two units. Any output beyond that stays unused. For example, five active systems sharing eight units each receive 1.6 units and run at 80% performance. Passive rooms, generators, and the command console do not consume this supply.
- **Small Power Generators** can appear in limited trader stock through the normal parts catalog and **Systems** filter. Each costs around 35 metal, occupies two adjacent cells, has 65 base health, and adds two shared power units without requiring crew or consuming power. The normal room upgrades add one output unit per level, up to four at level 3. Multiple installed generators stack with reactor output. Partial damage does not reduce generation; disabling a generator removes its output immediately, and repairing it restores output. Generators can be rotated and moved at traders like other rooms, and persist in the hold, hull, and market stock without restocking sold-out offers. The power panel shows total supply and the reactor/generator breakdown; the inspector shows each generator’s current output.
- Adding or disabling a system immediately recalculates everyone's equal share. There are no manual allocations or damage-limited power bars. Partial room damage does not change output or allocation; a destroyed system drops out of sharing, and a destroyed command core stops its power supply and ends the run.
- Lower shared power slows weapon reload, reduces engine force and torque, and slows shield recharge. It does not reduce a shield's maximum strength. Weapon, engine, and shield room upgrades improve their normal outputs without demanding additional power.
- **Upgrade Reactor** in System Power adds two output units for 40 metal at reactor level 1, then costs 20 more metal for each further level. Reactor level is a ship property, separate from command-room level, and has no fixed upgrade limit. It can be upgraded from the ship view, sector map or trader outside combat. The command inspector’s **Reinforce Room** adds 25 hull health per room level and does not add reactor power. Reactor upgrades do not reinforce or heal the command room. Existing command-core power upgrades and older reactor upgrades migrate once into the separate reactor without losing purchased output; old manual allocations are discarded.
- A crew member operating an engine boosts that engine's force by 25% after reaching its console. A pilot at the command console enables evasion while engines receive power: 5% base, plus 3% per engine power unit and 5% per staffed engine, capped at 45%. Each approaching enemy projectile rolls once; an evaded shot passes both shields and hull and shows **MISS**. No pilot or no functioning engines means no evasion. Only one crew bonus applies to each engine or shield room.

- Engines come in three sizes. **Compact Ion Engine** uses one cell and produces 110 base thrust for 22 metal; **Standard Ion Engine** keeps the original three-cell column and 270 thrust for 35 metal; **Heavy Ion Engine** uses a 2×3 room and produces 620 thrust for 70 metal. Their base health is 50, 80, and 125. Each consumes the same automatic share of power, reaching full output at two units; upgrades add 20% base thrust per level, and an operator adds 25%. Output supplies turning torque and contributes to evasion; placement and orientation still matter. Single, twin, and triple exhaust nozzles distinguish the sizes. Existing engines remain standard engines with their saved positions, health, and upgrades.
- Traders may carry any of the three engine sizes and show footprint and base thrust on each offered card. The engine inspector shows actual current force alongside its base output. Returning traders preserve their offers, sold quantities, and prices. Every size reserves its entire rear edge and an extra cell behind its nozzle; new rooms cannot block the exhaust. All sizes support engine crew assignments, evasion, sector jumps, and engine-related encounters.

## Combat facing controls

- The player ship holds its position. Use the facing dial, Q/E, or the mouse wheel to order a new angle; the selected and current angles are shown separately.
- Turning takes time. Each engine applies torque according to its mounted thrust direction and position. Thrusters fire only when a turn needs them. If the installed engines can turn only one way, the ship turns the long way around.
- Click an enemy ship or one of its live rooms to focus automatic fire. Select a crew member and right click a floor cell to order movement inside either ship when applicable.
- Prevent the browser's context menu from interrupting right-click orders during play.

## Crew

- Ships have a persistent crew roster that can grow through trading. Each trader offers one or two named recruits for 40–55 metal. **Crew** in the market filters to recruitment cards. Hiring consumes that offer once, places the new crew member at the command console, and preserves their identity, color, position, and later job in saves. There is no fixed three-person crew limit.
- Every crew member can be assigned to pilot, operate engines, weapons or shields, repair systems, deliver ammo or drones, or stand by. Jobs become effective at their system stations; recruits use the same movement and job rules as the starting crew.
- Assigning work reserves an available matching station, including while its operator is walking there. Weapons, engines, shields, and the pilot console each take one operator. Current operators and valid reservations stay in place; new assignments choose the nearest reachable unclaimed station. Extra crew wait at the command room until a station opens. Reassignment, disabled or removed systems, and completed repairs release reservations; saved jobs keep their valid station choices when loading.
- Repair crews spread across different damaged rooms, prioritizing the most damaged unclaimed room. Loaders reserve different ammunition-consuming weapons throughout pickup and delivery; they may share an ammo source. A loader with no available destination waits without generating ammunition.
- Crew assigned to shield operation walk to a shield console and increase its recharge rate when stationed there.
- Crew can perform support tasks, including delivering ammunition to weapons that require it.
- Crew walk along connected floor cells to system stations. An assignment becomes effective when the crew member reaches the relevant station.

## Crew health and direct orders

- Every person has 100 health. A hull hit injures living crew in the struck room for 1.5 times the hull damage, after shields. Enemy crew and your boarding party also take this damage from hits on their room. Crew at zero health die, stop all work, and remain recorded as dead in saves; natural healing does not revive them. New recruits arrive at full health. Older saves gain health without replacing existing crew.
- Living people aboard your own ship regenerate three health per second after five seconds without damage. They do not regenerate while boarding. Health appears on crew cards and wounded sprites. Time stops while an event overlay or the main menu is open.
- Click a person on the ship or their portrait to select them. Right click a reachable floor cell to walk there, in the hangar or on the rotated combat ship. A manual order pauses that person's automatic job and releases their reserved station; **Resume Assigned Job** or changing their assignment restores automatic work. **Escape** or **Focus My Ship** leaves crew selection. Ships hold position during combat.

Dead crew immediately disappear from assignment cards and cannot be selected; their saved records prevent them from returning on reload.

## Boarding and drones

- Traders sell a four-cell **Crew Teleporter** for about 75 metal a three-cell **Drone Bay** for about 65 metal, and a four-cell **Drone Assembly** for about 50 metal. All three share reactor power as active systems and reach full performance at two power units. Each market, including older saved markets, adds missing system offers without restocking sold ones.
- Select a living person, then **Board Team (Up to 2)** on a target ship. That person and one nearby available crewmate walk to the teleporter and depart together when both reach the pad. The target must be within 600 units, the pad must be powered, and its 12-second recharge must be complete. Recharge speed follows its shared power. Single-person orders are supported by the same simulation. People away from the ship do not operate their old systems.
- The combat camera follows a selected boarder. Right click enemy floor cells, or use the room buttons, to move around that ship's connected rooms. Boarders deal 10 crew damage per second to a nearby defender; each defender deals eight per second. With no nearby defender, a stationary boarder sabotages the current room for five system damage per second. Sabotage does not hurt its own attacker. Raiders have two 70-health defenders. Defeating every defender with an assault aboard captures the ship and wins the encounter, with 12 extra metal for the intact prize.
- **Recall Crew** returns the selected person when a functioning teleporter is recharged and the target is within range. Surviving crew automatically return when their target is destroyed, the battle ends, or the player exits combat to the main menu. Reloading an interrupted combat save also returns surviving boarders; their saved injuries and deaths remain.
- Drones use built ship systems. Each **Drone Assembly** builds drones of its configured **Combat**, **Beam**, **Defense**, **Repair**, or **Boarding** design while the player is in the ship view, at a trader, or in combat. Assembly production, drone delivery, and repair-drone activity stop completely on the sector map, including while a beacon event is open. Work resumes from the same progress when the player returns to the ship or enters combat. A base assembly takes four seconds of full power per 60-health drone, with a 25% build-speed bonus per room upgrade. Partial power slows production; a disabled or manually paused assembly stops production. Changing its design outside combat resets the current build progress but keeps every completed drone. There is no fleet-size cap, drone-parts cost, launch cost, or manual deployment step. The sector map shows loaded and waiting counts with a paused status.
- **Drone Bays** have no operating-slot limit. They have a directional door on their exterior edge. Construction reserves the first two empty launch cells and requires an unobstructed straight lane from the door to open space. Later rooms cannot cap the lane, even beyond the first two cells. Existing interior drone-control rooms retain their shape and become bays; they show a blocked-launch status until moved to a valid hull edge. Their presence does not block unrelated construction while waiting for a refit.
- Finished drones queue at their assembly for physical delivery. Crew assigned **Deliver ammo / drones** handle drone deliveries after their ammunition work; **Deliver drones** prioritizes drone transport. A carrier reserves one completed drone and a reachable, clear bay, walks to the assembly, picks up that exact drone, visibly carries it along connected floor cells, and loads it only after reaching the bay console. Without crew, a route, or a clear powered bay, output stays at the assembly. Multiple carriers cannot duplicate drones. Repair drones require the same delivery before they can begin work.
- Manual movement retains carried drone cargo while pausing the delivery. Switching to an unrelated job, boarding, or a carrier's death drops the drone in that person's current floor cell, where another loader can collect it. Carried identity, pickup reservations and loaded bay state survive saving and reloading. Survivors dock back at their bay between battles without needing another factory delivery.
- Loaded spacegoing drones launch automatically at battle start, or after a crew member finishes loading an available powered bay. They travel out through the visible door along its clear launch lane until outside the ship, then begin their role. Combat and beam drones fly continuously to the selected living enemy, then circle outside its shield in either direction. Target changes or a destroyed target send them toward another living enemy without teleporting. Their approach routes avoid their own ship. Defense drones orbit the player’s ship. Boarding drones approach a target within 600 units before entering it, fighting and sabotaging, then automatically move to another functioning room. Repair drones leave the bay into the ship's corridors, walk to damaged rooms and repair at four health per second, including outside combat. Every role requires a functioning bay with a clear launch lane.
- Combat drones fire six-damage laser bolts every 1.6 seconds while close to the enemy (within its collision radius plus 220 units), waiting for a clear line past their own hull. Beam drones make a one-second left-to-right sweep every three seconds, at up to 400 units, dealing nine damage per exposed room or three shield strength per room. They use the same beam collision and shield rules as ship weapons. Bolts hit actual rooms. Defense drones intercept one nearby incoming projectile every 0.8 seconds. Boarding drone crew damage and sabotage match a human boarder. Shared bay power scales drone movement, action rates, and repairs. Disabled or obstructed bays pause their drones. Internal drones and boarders take room-hit damage; spacegoing drones can be hit by enemy projectiles.
- Surviving drones keep their health and return ready for the next encounter. Assembly designs, build progress, pause state, and all completed drones persist in saves. Older saves discard obsolete raw drone parts. The **Drones & Boarding** panel shows built, waiting, and loaded counts for each assembly, its next build progress, a pause/resume button, and each bay's clear-lane status; room inspectors show design and launch status. Every trader includes a Drone Bay and Drone Assembly offer alongside its random stock. Missing essentials are added to older saved markets; existing quantities and prices, including sold-out drone offers, remain unchanged on reopening and reloading. The **Drones** shop filter shows these two rooms, which also remain in **Systems**. Crew Teleporters are available through random system stock.

## Ammunition production and logistics

- Ammunition is persistent and typed. Heavy ammo serves railguns, flak, siege cannons and Gauss repeaters. Missile ammo serves missile racks, swarm launchers and torpedoes. Each trigger consumes one round per projectile, including the whole salvo or timed burst; each magazine holds four triggers. Energy and light-shot weapons are ammo-free, including the Heavy Laser.
- A two-cell **Ammo Assembly** costs around 45 metal and participates in trader stock and the Systems filter. Its inspector selects **Heavy** (four rounds per batch) or **Missile** (two rounds per batch). A batch takes four seconds at full shared power; upgrades add 25% production speed per level. Switching recipes resets unfinished progress and preserves finished stock of both types. Each output type holds up to 24 rounds. Factories use shared energy only, with no raw supplies, metal charge or other consumable input. Disabled or unpowered factories and full output stop production. Production runs while playing, including on the map, and pauses in menus and event overlays.
- Factories hold their own output. Recovered finished ammunition is held in shared stores, physically collected at the command core. A loader reserves a reachable weapon with magazine space, walks to a reachable source of the correct type, removes up to four actual rounds, visibly carries a colored pack, then adds those rounds only to that weapon's magazine. Competing loaders cannot duplicate stock. Full magazines release their reservations. With no valid destination, leftover cargo is carried back to the core. Manual movement and other jobs pause delivery without deleting carried rounds. A destroyed factory's stock is unavailable until repaired.
- Every recovered crate immediately gives **eight heavy rounds and four missile rounds**. Typed stores, factory stock/progress, carried ammunition and magazine contents survive saves, scene changes and battles. Starting a battle never refills ammunition or consumes a crate counter.
- Existing saves receive the starting ammunition kit once, and each old sealed crate is unpacked once using the same crate contents. Old weapon magazines start empty. Repeated loads do not refill ammunition or magazines. Existing saves discard the obsolete raw-supply counter and trader supply packs while preserving finished rounds, factory progress, magazines and carried ammo. The **Ammo Production & Delivery** panel shows recovered stock, each factory's output/progress and every magazine, with empty/offline feedback; cards and inspectors show the exact ammo type and trigger cost.

- **Ammo Holds are removed** from new ships, trader stock and the purchase catalog. Loaders collect recovered rounds at the command core and manufactured rounds directly at factories. Existing installed holds become armor while preserving their ID, footprint (including rotation and mirroring), position, health and level, so crew routes and hull connections remain intact. Held uninstalled holds become held armor; old market offers are removed without restocking other offers. Migration runs once per hold and preserves stored ammunition.

## Resources and progression

- **Metal** is the main resource. It is used chiefly for system upgrades and trading.
- Encounters can provide metal, ammunition, rescued crew, or intact weapons. Recovered weapons enter the hold for installation at a trader. A hauler mechanic or stranded medic joins the persistent crew after rescue. New runs begin with a spare ion blaster instead of a spare armor room; older saves keep their existing inventory.
- Trading and upgrades should feed back into ship construction and readiness for future events.
- Sector charts spread beacons across a two-dimensional field, with connected branches and loops; their links are bidirectional. Players may revisit beacons, including traders and repair outposts, without replaying one-time rewards. The exit stays in the current chart until the player chooses to advance; there is no pursuing fleet.
- A chart contains one trader, one outpost, and distinct event sites including pirate raids, freighter rescues, salvage, derelicts, anomalies, relays, minefields, and lifeboats. Different scenes offer different choices. Before choosing, buttons describe an action and any known upfront cost; they do not list guaranteed rewards, net gains, crew or weapon prizes, exact damage, or repair amounts. Scene descriptions provide visible clues and risks without spelling out the result. Results are revealed only after the player acts, through the outcome toast, changed ship state, and captain’s log. Lifeboat passengers travel aboard until delivered to an outpost for payment; salvaged ammo crates immediately add persistent typed ammunition; restored relays and gravity lenses open new two-way links. Traders do not pay for uncollected survey data.

## Open design choices

Longer-term combat balance, additional events and resources, more module types, deeper crew controls, and progression beyond the current sector loop are still to be decided. Develop these incrementally and record new rules here.

## Current playable version

- Combat runs in real time with 22 purchasable weapon designs. Weapons differ in arc, damage, reload time, ammunition per trigger, projectile speed, and effects. Pulse cannons, autocannons, Gatling batteries, scatter cannons, plasma/cryo/inferno projectors, lasers, ion blasters, disruptors, Tesla coils, proton lances and sweeping beams use no ammunition. Railguns, flak, siege cannons and Gauss repeaters consume heavy ammo; missile racks, swarm launchers and torpedoes consume missile ammo from their individual magazines, waiting for loaders when empty. Catalogs and inspectors show the cost. Salvos share one trigger’s ammo cost. Shots start at their muzzle and can reach across the fixed exchange. Direct fire respects its mounted arc and is blocked by hull cells, with the proton lance exception below.
- Missile racks and swarm launchers automatically acquire living enemies regardless of target bearing. They launch along a clear direction within their mount’s launch arc, stay straight until they clear the hull’s projected edge, and then home toward their target. Guided missiles reacquire the nearest living enemy if their target is destroyed. They still consume ammunition. Missile racks, swarm rockets and torpedoes bypass shields rather than depleting them, including their splash damage. Torpedoes remain heavy unguided explosives. Each Railgun and Gauss slug hits up to two aligned rooms and never damages the same room twice. Those rooms may be on one enemy or separate enemies. An active projected enemy shield stops the slug even if excess damage reaches the first room. Proton lances pass harmlessly through the firing ship’s own hull and stop at the first enemy.
- Ion hits halve the efficiency of the system nearest the impact for three seconds: weapon and core hits reduce firing efficiency, while engine hits reduce enemy evasion. Each enemy has independent system status. Cryo effects slow enemy weapon reloads; burning still damages rooms over time. A gunner at any weapon station improves its fire rate.
- The disruptor is a broad, weaker alternative to the ion blaster: a 155° firing arc, 5 direct damage, and an 80-unit pulse that reduces every live functional system touched by the pulse to 80% efficiency for two seconds. It can affect several rooms on the struck ship and nearby ships, does not slow the entire ship, and does not damage secondary rooms.
- Tesla coils deal 6 damage to the struck system, then arc to up to three other live functional systems on that same ship. Each hop travels at most 110 units between system centers and deals 70% of the previous hit’s damage (4.2, 2.94, 2.058 at level 1). A system is hit at most once per discharge. Bounces skip armor and disabled rooms, stop when the ship is destroyed, and never jump to another ship. Visible arcs follow the ship’s rotated system positions.
- An engineer repairs a damaged module after walking to its station. A loader walks between an ammo source and a weapon to deliver ammunition.
- The default Wayfarer has a command core, two standard forward engines, and two mirrored pulse cannons facing forward. Its occupied cells and external mounts are symmetric above and below the centerline, and each engine pair has equal opposite turning torque. Arsenal and Bulwark offer missile and heavy-ammo production loadouts in the starting lineup. An untouched legacy starter save gains the mirrored cannon automatically; customized or damaged ships are preserved. It begins with one spare ion blaster in the hold. All 22 weapons, engines, wide and focused shield arrays, small power generators, ammo assemblies and armor plating can be bought and installed at traders. Each new trader draws four to eight random module units with replacement from all buyable designs. Weapons, engines and shields can be absent. A missing Drone Bay or Drone Assembly is added as one extra offer at its normal price, making the complete drone production pair available without relying on random draws. Repeated types share a card with their total quantity. Traders also offer one or two recruits. Returning traders keep their remaining parts, prices, and recruit availability; older saved markets gain only missing drone essentials without replenishing sold offers. Modules must connect by an edge and cannot overlap or block reserved exhaust and mount space.
- During construction, a selected module stays in placement mode when the cursor is over an installed room. The preview and click snap to a nearby valid edge position, including below the ship. Without a selected catalog module, clicking a room inspects it. Leaving a trader clears placement mode. A trader at the current beacon can be reopened with **Dock at Trader** from the ship view.
- Pirate ships are assembled from connected four-cell tetromino modules, with visible core, weapon, engine, shield, and sometimes armor rooms. Each room has its own health and function. Combat framing uses their actual tile count.
- A destroyed command core ends the run. Other destroyed modules stop providing their function until repaired. Metal pays traders for new modules and pays for upgrades, repairs, and some encounter choices.
- A generated sector chart has connected, revisitable beacons and a dedicated exit. Pirate and some freighter destinations lead to fixed ship combat; salvage, anomalies, derelicts, minefields, lifeboats, relays, outposts, and traders offer other choices. Completed sites remain on the chart. A new chart appears only when the player chooses **Go to Next Sector** at the exit.
- Completing an encounter choice, a combat victory, an outpost repair or survivor delivery immediately returns to the sector map. A toast announces the result and the captain's log retains its details; there is no follow-up map-versus-shipyard prompt. The map's shipyard control remains available.
- Progress is stored in the browser's local storage. The game is a static site with no account or server dependency.
- The game opens at a title menu. **Continue Game** loads the local run when its command core is intact; **New Game** replaces it after confirmation. The in-game **Main Menu** action saves and returns to the title screen. **Quit Game** ends the browser session at a closing screen with a path back to the menu; an unfinished battle is abandoned and can be attempted again from its chart beacon.

## Weapon fitting and firing patterns

The 22 weapons use ten footprint patterns. Six compact designs occupy only one or two cells. Catalog cards and inspectors show the occupied-cell diagram, cell count, dimensions, and behavior details derived from the combat settings. Rocket pods show recessed loaded rockets, torpedo tubes show a large warhead, and electrical coils and laser emitters have different silhouettes. Projectiles likewise distinguish rockets with exhaust, laser bolts, ion pulses, plasma, and incendiary shots.

Existing installed weapons keep their saved footprint and muzzle location, preserving ship layouts. While docked at a trader, **Refit to New Footprint · Free** previews the new room shape. Only confirming a valid location changes the room; its identity, health, upgrades, range, and crew are preserved. Canceling retains the original room.

Gatling bursts fire three rounds 0.09 seconds apart; Gauss repeaters fire two slugs 0.12 seconds apart; swarm launchers fire four guided rockets 0.10 seconds apart. Later rounds launch from the weapon's current location and track the target between shots. Queued rounds cancel if their weapon is disabled or their target is no longer valid. Trigger ammo is paid once for the complete burst. Flak and scatter salvos fire simultaneously.

Sweeping beams are continuous rays anchored to the moving mount, with the barrel following the cut from left to right. Each exposed enemy room takes one hit per sweep, independent of frame rate; the first live room blocks rooms behind it along that ray. The firing ship blocks the ray, and each direction obeys the weapon’s mounted arc. Shielded room hits remove one third of the listed hull damage from shield strength. An intact shield prevents hull and crew damage; if it collapses during a hit, only the unabsorbed fraction returns to full hull strength (an 18-damage cut against 3 shield strength leaves 9 hull damage). Multiple shields absorb in turn. Disabled or removed emitters, unavailable bay power, and blocked launch lanes end an active beam. Beam drones require the same assembly, physical crew delivery and bay launch as other spacegoing drones.
